## [EXECUTION PLAN]

### Intent

Two contract migrations close out earlier epics: the unread
`war_week.organizer_emails` (ticket 18) and `competition.bracket_points`
with its enum (ticket 19) leave the schema, now that Paul has confirmed no
rollback goes past Epics B and E. Then the Bracket gains its third kind of
Entrant, the **Squad** (a named group of Participants from one Team, entered
as one Entrant of a team-scoring Competition, its Placement Points going to
its Team), and its first non-Organizer write: **self-report**, where a
Participant who is in a Heat enters its result from their phone and it
counts at once, exactly like a Host-entered Heat Result. There is no
confirmation step and no pending state: a Host who doesn't trust reports
turns self-report off, and the Host or an Organizer can still overwrite any
result on the results screen. No new role, no new Format: the engine
(`src/lib/bracket/engine.ts`, `heats.ts`) does not change; a report goes
through the same `resetByResult` / `applyResult` / `saveBracket` path as
`recordHeatResult`, by construction (one shared core).

### Decisions resolved for this plan

Each is the planner's reading of the contract; Paul may overrule any in the
epic file before `/atlas-implement`. The epic's decisions 1–14 are
confirmed; this plan lists under "Open decisions for Paul" only what it
adds.

1. **Two migrations, drops first (epic Order 1).** 0013 is the two drops
   and nothing else, so its SQL is exactly three statements and its
   rollback story is the one Paul confirmed. 0014 is additive: new tables, a
   nullable `entrant.squad_id`, two nullable `heat` columns, a defaulted
   boolean, and the replaced check constraint (the new check accepts every
   row the old one did). **Rollback and the deploy window:** both
   migrations ship in one PR, so there is no build between them. The build
   before this one (Epic F) still declares both dropped columns and
   `src/queries/war-weeks.ts` runs `select().from(warWeek)`, so once 0013
   runs on production every page of the F build fails until the G build is
   live; `migrate.yml` runs on the push to `main` separately from Vercel's
   deploy, so that window is real (a few minutes), and Instant Rollback to
   the F build is gone from then on. Paul's tickets 18/19 confirmation
   covers rolling back past Epics B and E, not F; so the `staging → main`
   promotion is a human gate (below): Paul promotes at a quiet time (no War
   Week is live before February 2027), knowing there is no Instant Rollback
   to F, and checks production afterwards. Preview deployments of older
   branches also break against the migrated staging database. 0014 on its
   own is rollback-safe (the old code never reads the new tables or
   columns). The drops still go first in the branch so their proof stands
   alone before any Squad code lands.
2. **`src/db/migration-copy.test.ts` is deleted**, not rewritten: it
   inserts `organizer_emails` fixtures and reads the column back after
   `migrate()` runs every later migration, so it cannot pass past 0013,
   and ticket 18's criterion forbids the name anywhere under `src/`. The
   `src/lib/setup.test.ts` case "never carries Organizer emails" goes the
   same way (the neighbouring `status` case already proves the settings
   schema strips unknown keys). Every other `organizerEmails` hit is a
   `WarWeek` test fixture, removed mechanically.
3. **A Squad belongs to a Team (`squad.team_id`).** Decision 1 ("all its
   Participants are on the same Team") and decision 4 ("its Placement
   Points go to its Team") both need the Team on the row: the Points Entry
   target is `squad.team_id`, never recomputed from its Participants, so two Squads
   of one Team each earn their own entry and a later roster change can't
   move a Squad's points. Creating or editing a Squad refuses a Participant
   whose `team_id` differs from the Squad's ("Every Participant in a Squad
   must be on the same {Team Label}."). To keep that true afterwards,
   `updateParticipant` refuses a Team change (a different `teamId`, or
   none) while the Participant is in a Squad, and `deleteParticipant` and
   `deleteTeam` count Squads in their `inUseError`, whose shape is fixed
   ("This Participant has 2 Squads. Remove them from the Squads before
   changing their Team." / "… Delete them or remove the Participant from
   them first."), decision 3 of the epic. `updateCompetition` refuses a
   scoring change and `setCompetitionFormat` a change to `points` while
   the Competition has Squads ("This Competition has 2 Squads. Delete them
   before changing its scoring." / "… its Format."), so a Squad never
   outlives the kind of Competition that can enter it. Locks: a Squad write
   selects its Participants `for share` and `updateParticipant` (like
   `deleteParticipant` today) locks the row `for update`, so a Team change
   and a Squad write on the same Participant serialize. A Squad has 1–16
   Participants (a lone Participant is allowed: an odd-numbered Team still
   fields a Squad), a name of 1–80 characters unique within the Competition
   (case-sensitive, like Team names; a duplicate is refused before the
   unique index: "A Squad named "X" already exists."), and a Participant is
   in at most one Squad per Competition ("Ashley Schuliger is already in
   Red Alpha."), checked in the mutation under the Competition row lock
   (`lockedCompetition`), which serializes every Squad write of one
   Competition; the database has no cross-row constraint for it. A seed
   reload can still delete a Squad's Participant (the `squad_participant`
   row cascades) or move them to another Team; CONTEXT.md's seed rules say
   so ("Squads aren't in seeds; a reload that removes or moves a
   Participant leaves their Squads to the Organizer"), the same caveat the
   roster already carries.
4. **Squads are an Entrant kind, never mixed.** `replaceEntrants` takes
   `kind` ("team" | "participant" | "squad"; omitted keeps today's rule,
   the scoring's kind, so every existing caller and the smoke loop are
   unchanged). `entrantKindError(scoring, kind)` refuses `squad` or `team`
   on an individual Competition and `participant` on a team one; every
   `targetId` must be a Squad of this Competition ("Enter Squads of this
   Competition."). Since one write sets the whole list under one kind, a
   mixed list is impossible by construction; the pure test covers the
   parser and `entrantKindError`, and the DB check (`num_nonnulls … = 1`)
   is the backstop per row. Deleting a Squad that is an Entrant is refused
   ("Remove it from the Entrants first."), like a Team or Participant;
   editing a Squad's name or Participants while it is an Entrant is allowed
   (the Entrant id, and so the Heats, are untouched); every Squad write is
   refused while the Bracket is finalized (`FINALIZED`). **Scoping:**
   `updateSquad` and `deleteSquad` match the Squad by `id and
   competition_id` under `lockedCompetition`, so a Host of Competition A
   posting A's id with B's Squad id gets "That Squad no longer exists." and
   nothing changes; the report path finds its Heat inside the locked
   Competition's own Bracket (`bracket.heats`), so a Heat id of another
   Competition is `HEAT_NOT_FOUND` ("That Heat no longer exists."); DB
   tests and a smoke case prove both.
5. **Seeding by Standings isn't offered for Squads** (decision 5): the
   builder hides "By Standings" when the Entrants are Squads, and
   `generateBracket` refuses `seeding: "standings"` when any Entrant is a
   Squad ("Squads are seeded at random."). `standingsSeedPositions` is
   untouched.
6. **`BracketEntrant` grows, `Entrant` doesn't.** The engine's `Entrant`
   (`id, seedPosition, label`) is unchanged. The query type gains
   `squadId: string | null`, `participantNames: string[]` (the Squad's
   Participants' display names by name; `[]` for a Team or Participant), `pointsTeamId:
   string | null` (`teamId` for a Team, `squad.teamId` for a Squad, null
   for a Participant) and keeps `teamId` as the *row's* `team_id` (null
   for a Squad), so `entrantForYou`'s team match can't pick the first Squad
   of Your Team. `label` is the Squad name; `color` and `teamName` are the
   Squad's Team's. `finalizeBracket` writes `teamId: pointsTeamId,
   participantId` per awarded Entrant (a Participant's `pointsTeamId` is
   null, so nothing changes for individual Brackets). `BracketCompetition`
   (`queries/brackets.ts`) selects `selfReport`, so `getBracket` carries it
   to the builder and both pages. `getTimedHeats` (`queries/schedule.ts`)
   left-joins `squad` for its Entrant labels, so a timed Squad Heat reads
   "Red Alpha vs Blue Bravo" in Now/Next, never "Unknown" (a DB test case
   in `queries/schedule.test.ts`). `getPointsEntryFormOptions` participants
   gain `teamId` (additive) for the Squad form's Team filter.
7. **You rules for a Squad (decision 6).** `entrantForYou(entrants, you,
   scoring)` takes `you: { participantId, teamId, squadId }`; when
   `you.squadId` matches an Entrant's `squadId` that Entrant is You; when
   the Bracket's Entrants are Squads and You aren't in one, null (Your Team
   isn't entered as such); otherwise today's rule. The competition page
   passes `participantSquads = getParticipantSquadIds(competitionId)` (a
   `Record<participantId, squadId>`) beside `participantTeams`.
8. **Reporting is one rule in `can`, with a Heat facet.** `AccessTarget`
   gains `heatReport?: { selfReport: boolean; heat: "open" | "unfilled" |
   "decided" | "bye" | "missing"; linked: { participantId, teamId, squadId
   } | null; entrants: { teamId, participantId, squadId }[] }` (`linked` is
   the Participant of the Competition's War Week whose email matches the
   actor's, ignoring case — account linking, never the pick; `squadId` is
   their Squad in this Competition; `entrants` are the Heat's filled slots'
   Entrants, where **`teamId` is the Entrant row's `team_id` — null for a
   Squad — sourced like `BracketEntrant.teamId` (decision 6), never
   `pointsTeamId`**, so in a Red Alpha vs Red Bravo Heat a Red Participant
   in neither Squad is not "in" it). `can(actor, "bracket.heat-report", target)` runs **before**
   the Organizer shortcut, so the Heat facts bind everyone, and returns in
   this order: `SIGN_IN_REFUSAL` (anonymous, **or an actor whose email
   fails `isJahnelGroupEmail`**: defence in depth for the first Participant
   write, since a non-JG session already counts as anonymous upstream);
   `ADMIN_REFUSAL` ("Organizers and Hosts only.", the programming-error
   path) when the `heatReport` facet is missing (a caller that forgot to
   load it can never grant); then `heatReportError(facet)` from
   `heat-report-rule.ts`: "Self-report is off for this Competition.";
   "Your sign-in doesn't match a Participant of this War Week."; "That Heat
   no longer exists." (`missing`); "You're not in this Heat." (no Entrant
   with `participantId === linked.participantId`, `teamId ===
   linked.teamId` (non-null) or `squadId === linked.squadId` (non-null));
   "A bye isn't played."; "This Heat is still waiting for its Entrants.";
   "This Heat already has a result." (`decided`: epic decision 10, which
   also covers the race); else null. `heatReportError` is the pure
   Heat-fact rule; `can` owns who-is-asking (sign-in, domain, facet
   present) and delegates the facts to it, and the mutation re-runs
   `heatReportError` (a business rule, not auth: ADR 0001) under the lock.
   The test table includes a non-JG actor **with** a matching `linked`
   (refused by the domain check, proving the domain rule and not the
   linkage one). Changing a result stays `recordHeatResult` under its
   existing action (the default branch: an Organizer or the row's Host), so
   a Participant can never overwrite: epic decision 10. The toggle is
   `"competition.self-report"` and Squad writes are `"bracket.squads"`,
   both the default branch.
9. **`authorizeHeatReport` keeps ADR 0003's order.** A new export next to
   `authorize`: actor → `isUuid(competitionId)` ("That Competition no
   longer exists.") → `isUuid(heatId)` ("That Heat no longer exists.") →
   `loadCompetitionTarget` → `getHeatReportFacts(competitionId, heatId,
   actor.email)` (one query set: the Competition's `self_report`, the
   Bracket via `loadBracket` for the Heat's state, the Heat's Entrants'
   ids, the linked Participant with their Team and Squad in this
   Competition, matched by `lower(participant.email) = lower($email)`
   within the Competition's War Week and treated as unlinked if more than
   one row comes back, defensively, since raw-SQL fixtures skip the
   write-time lowercasing) → `can` → `{ ok, ctx, warWeek, linked }`. The action parses
   `parseHeatResultInput` only after that, so a Participant with malformed
   input on an off Competition gets the access refusal (smoke asserts it).
10. **A report is a Heat Result with a reporter, through one shared core.**
    `recordHeatResult`'s transaction body moves into an exported
    `writeHeatResult(tx, found, heatId, result, reporter: { email,
    participantId } | null)` in `mutations/brackets.ts` (lock already held;
    `bracketRefusal`, `bracketOf`, find the Heat in the locked Bracket,
    `resetByResult` + `applyResult` with `BracketError` → refusal,
    `saveBracket`, return `{ ok, resetHeatIds }`); `recordHeatResult`
    becomes lock + `writeHeatResult(…, null)` with no behaviour change (its
    existing tests pass unchanged). `submitHeatReport(competitionId, heatId,
    result, ctx)` in `mutations/heat-reports.ts` takes the lock
    (`lockedCompetition`), reloads the facts inside the transaction with
    `getHeatReportFacts(…, tx)`, runs `heatReportError` again (so of two
    racing phones the second sees `decided` and gets "This Heat already has
    a result."), then calls `writeHeatResult(tx, found, heatId, result, {
    email: ctx.actorEmail, participantId: linked.participantId })`. So a
    report advances Entrants **exactly** as a Host result does: the same
    code. **Reporter columns:** `saveBracket(tx, before, after, reporter:
    { heatId, email, participantId } | null)` already rewrites every
    *changed* Heat (its JSON differs: status or slots; a report moves its
    Heat from ready to played, so the reported Heat is always in the set);
    it now sets `reported_by_email` / `reported_by_participant_id` on each
    changed Heat to the reporter's for `reporter.heatId`, and to null for
    every other changed Heat. That keeps the audit truthful without a
    separate cleanup: a Host save that *changes* a reported Heat's result
    clears its reporter (the result is now the Host's), and a Heat that a
    re-recorded earlier Heat refills or empties loses any reporter it had.
    A Host re-save of the identical result changes no Heat, so its
    "Reported by" line stays (the result is still the reporter's). Re-generating,
    replacing Entrants or changing Heat settings delete the Heats, and the
    columns go with them. Un-finalize doesn't touch them; finalizing writes
    Points Entries as today (their `createdBy` is the finalizer). The
    reporter's email is stored and never returned by any query that feeds
    a page or MCP: `loadBracket`'s `.select().from(heat)` becomes an
    explicit column list without the two reporter columns, so the email is
    never even read into those paths; `getHeatReporters(competitionId)`
    selects Heats with `reported_by_email is not null`, left-joins
    `reported_by_participant_id` to `participant.display_name`, and returns
    only the name or the fallback "a Participant" (the participant id is
    set null when that row is deleted, the email being retained for
    audit), never the email column's value; a DB test stringifies every returned
    query shape in `queries/brackets.ts` and `queries/heat-reports.ts` and
    asserts no "@".
11. **What each screen shows.** Results screen (`/admin/brackets/<id>`,
    Organizers and Hosts): a Heat whose reporter name is present shows
    "Reported by <name>" in small muted text on its card; its Sheet is the
    existing direct form unchanged ("Save" overwrites, resets later Heats
    with today's confirm, and clears the line). Participant view: the "Your
    next Heat" card, when the page's `selfReport.on`, the Heat is `open`,
    and `you.via === "email"` with `you.participantId ===
    selfReport.linkedParticipantId`, shows **Report result**, a bottom
    `Sheet` with the shared `WinnerForm` / `FinishingOrderForm` submitting
    `reportHeatResult` (with `confirmResets: false`: an open Heat has no
    later results to reset); success toast "Result reported."; the page
    refreshes and the Heat shows decided, the winner in the next Heat. A
    refusal (another phone got there first, or the Host turned self-report
    off) toasts the server's message and the Sheet stays open with the
    input kept. When self-report is on and You are known only by the pick
    (`via === "pick"`), Your next Heat carries one sentence: "To report
    results, ask an Organizer to add your email to the roster." The server
    decides what's reportable (the same `can` facet, computed once for the
